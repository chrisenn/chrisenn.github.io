Project

Channel Agent was an AI-powered experience in Microsoft Teams designed to help teams access information and complete work more efficiently within collaborative workflows.

As the product approached R0 (Ring 0), the team needed to understand whether the experience was ready for early adoption, identify barriers to usage, and gather insights that could inform future product direction.

Role

I led research planning and execution, facilitated cross-functional alignment workshops, conducted beta research with early users, synthesized findings, and translated insights into product recommendations.

Process

I combined cross-functional workshops and qualitative beta research to understand usability challenges, adoption barriers, and opportunities to improve the AI experience.

Alignment Workshop

I facilitated an end-to-end workshop with Product, Design, and Engineering partners to evaluate launch readiness and uncover risks that may not have been identified through AI-as-a-judge evaluation alone.

The workshop helped the team:

  • Align on launch readiness risks
  • Identify usability issues before release
  • Generate research questions for beta validation
  • Prioritize quick wins to improve the experience

Developing an AI Experience Evaluation Framework

As AI-powered experiences were still emerging, teams across Microsoft were approaching evaluation in different ways and measuring similar concepts through different frameworks. I synthesized existing approaches across teams, identified common themes, and created a unified evaluation framework for Channel Agent.

I made decisions about which dimensions were most relevant to the product experience and identified gaps that required additional research. I then incorporated product-specific measures based on previous generative research, including:

  • User utilization and frequency of Status Report usage
  • Perceived value and usefulness of generated outputs
  • Level of customization required to make outputs actionable
  • User confidence and trust in AI-generated information
  • Workflow fit and integration into existing team practices

This created a more consistent approach for evaluating AI quality and adoption, while ensuring the metrics reflected both user needs and product goals.

Beta Research

I conducted onboarding sessions and follow-up interviews with early users to understand how Channel Agent fit into existing team workflows.

Research explored:

  • Discoverability and onboarding
  • Mental models for interacting with AI
  • Trust and permissions
  • Collaboration dynamics
  • Collecting survey responses for our afore mentioned metric framework
  • Future workflow opportunities

Challenge

Channel Agent introduced a new AI interaction model within an existing collaboration environment. The team needed to understand not only whether the AI produced useful outputs, but whether users understood, trusted, and incorporated the experience into their workflows.

Key research questions included:

  • Would users understand the value of AI-generated Status Reports?
  • How would users incorporate the experience into their existing workflows?
  • What would build or reduce trust in an AI assistant operating on team knowledge?
  • What additional capabilities would create the most value in future iterations?

Research & Insights

1. Users needed clearer guidance for interacting with AI

Users struggled with discovering the experience, understanding how to get started, and knowing what types of prompts would produce useful results.

The AI capability was powerful, but users needed clearer onboarding and stronger mental models for how to collaborate with it.

Impact: Findings informed improvements to onboarding, messaging, and the overall experience design.

2. Trust and transparency influenced adoption

Users had concerns about permissions, knowledge sources, and what information the AI could access within collaborative spaces.

These findings highlighted that AI adoption depended not only on output quality, but also on users understanding how the system worked and feeling confident using it.

Impact: Research informed discussions around transparency, access, and user control within AI-powered workflows.

3. AI introduced new collaboration patterns

Because Channel Agent operated within shared team environments, users had questions about when to use it, who should contribute information, and how AI-generated outputs fit into team communication.

Impact: Findings helped shape conversations around creating lower-risk ways for users to experiment with AI and build confidence.

Impact

I synthesized research findings and shared them with stakeholders through research readouts and product discussions. The findings gained attention from senior leadership and helped drive conversations around improving the AI experience.

Research influenced product decisions including:

  • Creating a clearer 1:1 experience and sandbox environment to allow users to prompt more freely and avoid spamming their colleagues
  • Improving onboarding and guidance
  • Renaming the agent
  • Removing the Reviewers Chat and having a dedicated space
  • Informing future capabilities, including Planner integration and Scheduling

This project reinforced that successful AI products require more than technical capability. Users need to understand the experience, trust the system, and confidently integrate AI into their existing workflows.
